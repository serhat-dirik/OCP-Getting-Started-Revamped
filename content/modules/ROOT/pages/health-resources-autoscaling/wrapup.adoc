== What you built

You taught the platform three things about the claims service.
*When it can serve:* a readiness probe that held a broken rollout back and drained the Service to a `503` when no pod could answer, a liveness probe whose typo restarted a healthy container, and a startup probe that protects the boot.
*How much it needs:* explicit requests and limits, a QoS class you read back, and an `OOMKilled` container that showed what a too-small memory limit does.
*When to add more of it:* a HorizontalPodAutoscaler that grew the service from 2 to 4 pods under load and kept them through the lull.

The idea under all of it: the platform can only protect, place and scale an application that *tells it* what healthy looks like and what it needs.

== Map to your org

Take these back to how your teams work today:

. *What do your probes actually check?*
Pick one production service and read its liveness and readiness probes.
Does liveness reach a database, a cache, or another service?
If that dependency blipped right now, would the platform restart every pod at once?
. *Where did your requests come from?*
For the same service, compare its CPU and memory *requests* with what it actually uses on a normal day.
Were they measured, or copied from a template?
How much reserved-but-idle capacity is that across every replica and every environment?
. *Who scales your services at peak today?*
Is it a person, a ticket, a scheduled script, or the platform?
Which of your services rises and falls with traffic on CPU — a good HPA fit — and which is driven by a queue or sits idle for hours, where something else fits better?

== When *not* to do it this way

These are strong defaults, and each can be over-applied.
Be honest about the edges:

[cols="3,4",options="header"]
|===
|If… |Reach for…

|You are choosing what a liveness probe checks
|The process's *own* ability to make progress — never a dependency's health

|The load is driven by a queue or event backlog, not CPU
|KEDA (Custom Metrics Autoscaler), scaling on queue depth

|The service is idle for long stretches, then bursty
|Serverless (Knative), which scales to zero between bursts

|The app is single-instance by design (a leader, a singleton job) or holds local state
|A fixed replica count, not an HPA

|You do not know the app's real usage yet
|Measure first; leave the LimitRange defaults until you have numbers
|===

.The reasoning behind each row
[%collapsible]
====
* *A liveness probe that checks a dependency is a restart storm waiting to happen.*
When the database blips, every pod fails at once and the platform restarts all of them — turning a short outage into a long one, and hammering the database as it recovers.
Let readiness reflect dependencies if you must; keep liveness about the process itself.
* *CPU is the wrong signal for queue-driven work.*
A consumer that is waiting on a queue uses little CPU while the backlog grows; an HPA on CPU never notices.
Scale on the backlog instead.
* *An HPA never scales to zero.*
Its floor is at least one pod, so a service that is idle most of the day pays for that pod all day.
Serverless removes it.
* *Not every workload can run more copies of itself.*
A leader-elected controller, a singleton job or an app with local state may break or do duplicate work when an autoscaler adds replicas.
* *Guessed requests are worse than defaults.*
A request copied from a template reserves capacity nobody uses; a limit set too low gives you the `OOMKilled` you caused on purpose.
Measure under real load, then set both.
====
