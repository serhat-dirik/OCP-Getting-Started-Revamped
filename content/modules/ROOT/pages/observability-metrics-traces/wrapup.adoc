== What you built

You took the claims service from *running blind* to *observed*, using only capabilities that ship with OpenShift or install as operators under your existing subscription:

* *Metrics* — read the golden signals (rate, errors, latency) and a custom `claims_created_total` in *Observe → Metrics*, scraped into user-workload monitoring by a *ServiceMonitor*.
* *An alert* — a `PrometheusRule` in your namespace that you *made fire* by breaking the database, then recovered (database back *and* app restarted to drop the dead connection pool).
* *A dashboard as code* — a four-panel `PersesDashboard` (request rate, 5xx rate, latency, CPU per pod) in *Observe → Dashboards (Perses)*, changed by editing a file, reading the diff and re-applying.
* *A trace* — followed a request in *Observe → Traces*, ranked requests by duration, and saw the `trace_id` exemplar that links a metric back to one exact request.

== Map to your org

. *When your last incident started, how long until someone knew — and was it a customer who told you?*
You built an alert that pages on the 5xx rate *before* the error budget is gone.
For a service your team runs: is "it's down" detected by a metric and an alert, or by a support ticket?
What is the one golden-signal alert you would add on Monday that would have caught your last incident earlier?
. *Where do your team's dashboards live, and who could rebuild them if the cluster went away tonight?*
You wrote a dashboard as a file, changed it with a one-line diff, and re-applied it.
Are your on-call views hand-built in a UI that someone has to click together again on every new cluster, or reviewed and shipped like the services they watch?
. *In your last "is it us or the database?" bridge call, what would a trace have saved you?*
You watched metrics say *what* and a trace say *where*; the logs held the cause, but only the alert told you to read them.
Think of an incident that burned an hour locating the fault across services.
If every request carried a trace id you could search, how much of that hour disappears?

== When *not* to reach for this

Observability is powerful, which means being honest about their edges:

[cols="3,4"]
|===
|Don't… |Instead…

|Alert on causes: a pool at 80%, a queue at 100
|Alert on the few *symptoms* a user feels, like the 5xx rate; let metrics and traces explain the cause

|Build a dashboard panel for every metric the app exposes
|Chart the few questions on-call asks first; let *Observe → Metrics* answer the one-off ones
|===

.The reasoning behind each row
[%collapsible]
====
* *Don't alert on causes; alert on symptoms.*
You alerted on the *5xx rate* — a symptom your users feel.
It is tempting to alert on every internal metric (a pool at 80%, a queue at 100), but cause-alerts page constantly and train people to ignore the pager.
Alert on the handful of *symptoms* that mean a user is having a bad time, and let metrics and traces explain the cause once you are looking.
* *Don't build a dashboard panel for every metric.*
A dashboard is read in the first minute of an incident, so every panel competes for that minute.
Forty panels mean nobody finds the one that moved.
Chart the handful of questions on-call asks first (traffic, errors, latency, saturation, as you did) and leave the one-off questions to *Observe → Metrics*.
====

== Go deeper

* *Scale on what you just measured* — the HorizontalPodAutoscaler you built in xref:health-resources-autoscaling/lab.adoc[Health, Resources & Autoscaling] scales on the same CPU you graphed here; compare its replica count against these panels.
* *Keep the service up through maintenance* — PodDisruptionBudgets and node drains are exercised in xref:deployment-targets-scheduling/lab.adoc[Deployment Targets & Scheduling].
* *Logs at scale* — this workshop cluster does not run a shared log store by default (the app is deliberately quiet, and a shared log store needs object storage).
The always-available baseline is `oc logs` and the console's *Pod → Logs*; a platform team that needs centralized log aggregation could add an optional Loki tier and the Cluster Observability Operator's logging plugin once log volume justifies it — not something this module builds or demonstrates.
* *Per-query database spans* — enabling database-span instrumentation on the claims image turns the N+1 endpoint into a visible staircase of `SELECT` spans; tracked as an enhancement to the sample app.
