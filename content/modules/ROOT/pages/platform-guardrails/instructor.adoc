*Read this before you schedule the module.* Hands-on is *~35 min* estimated; with the concept page and wrap-up, budget *~50 min*.

The module has two halves that feel completely different in a room, and knowing which one you are in is most of delivering it well.
Exercises 1–2 are the secrets half: concrete, visual, and they end with a clock.
Exercises 3–4 are the policy half: textual and slower, and the payoff is a sentence on a screen rather than a state change.
Do not try to give them the same energy.

== The beat that needs handling: fifteen seconds of nothing

In exercise 2 the attendee writes to the vault and then watches a hash that does not change, twice, before it does.

That silence is the single most valuable moment in the module and it is also where a room gets restless. Say what is happening before it happens: *"the operator re-checks every fifteen seconds, so you are about to watch nothing happen twice."* Then let it be quiet.

If you fill the silence with talking, the moment the hash flips goes unnoticed, and the module's central claim lands as an assertion instead of an observation.

== What must not appear on screen

Exercise 1 has a `WARNING` block telling attendees not to run `-o jsonpath='{.data}'`. Someone will run it anyway.

That is recoverable and worth using: the value they just printed is a workshop fixture, not a real credential, and the base64 on their screen is the whole point — encoding is not encryption, and it just travelled into their scrollback. Make it the lesson rather than an incident. But do not demonstrate it yourself from the presenter machine, because a recording keeps it.

== Top questions attendees ask

*1. "Why can't I write my own policy? That's the interesting part."*::
Because a `ValidatingAdmissionPolicy` is cluster-scoped, and `--dry-run=server` is authorized as a real `create` — so even "just validating" one needs cluster-scoped write.
Granting every attendee that permission in order to practise guardrails would remove the guardrail.
Then land the honest version: in an organisation you author the policy and it reaches the cluster through review and GitOps, not because you had permission to apply it by hand. The workshop is not simplifying here; it is matching how this actually works.

*2. "The Secret changed but my app is still using the old password. Is that broken?"*::
No, and this is the best question in the module.
Environment variables are read once, at container start. The Secret changed; the process did not.
A Secret mounted as a volume is updated in place by the kubelet, and an app that re-reads the file picks it up without a restart.
Neither is wrong — but the difference is invisible until the day you rotate, which is exactly when you least want to discover it.

*3. "So the password IS in Git — I found the file."*::
They found `gitops/promotion/claims-config-template/base/claims-creds-secret.yaml`, and exercise 1 sends them there deliberately.
It belongs to a different module and it is the anti-pattern this one replaces: same name, same value, plaintext `stringData`.
The precise claim is that nothing in *this* module's chart creates `claims-creds` — their namespace's Secret exists only because the operator materialized it. Have them re-run the `grep` against `gitops/entry-states/platform-guardrails/templates/` and see the difference.

*4. "Couldn't I just read the vault directly and skip all this?"*::
They can, from inside the namespace, and that is worth acknowledging rather than dodging: this is a dev-mode OpenBao holding a fixture, with the token in a Secret next to it.
What the module is teaching is the *shape* — a value that lives in one system, a controller that derives a Kubernetes Secret from it, and a rotation that is a write rather than a project. Production differs in the vault's auth model, not in that shape.

*5. "What happens if the operator is down?"*::
The existing Secret keeps working — it is an ordinary object and nothing deletes it.
What stops is reconciliation: a rotation would not propagate, and you would not be told. That is worth naming, because "the sync silently stopped" is the realistic failure, not an outage.

== Failure modes you may hit live

*The ExternalSecret says `SecretSyncedError` for the first few seconds after prep.* Expected — MEASURED at 15 s on a fresh install, and it clears on the first successful reconcile. Do not start exercise 1 until `oc get externalsecret claims-creds` reports `SecretSynced`.

*A denial that names a rule the attendee thought they had satisfied.* A policy reports the first validation that evaluates false, in declaration order — it stops at the first failure. Exercise 4's first manifest breaks two rules on purpose and names only one; if someone fixes that and is refused again, the guardrail is working.

*Exercise 3 returns `Forbidden`.* The attendee-facing read of a cluster-scoped policy needs `admissionregistration.k8s.io` get/list/watch on `platform-observer`, which ships in `gitops/workshop-config`. If it is Forbidden, that grant did not reach the cluster — check the `workshop-config` Application is Synced rather than debugging the attendee's identity.

== If you are short on time

Cut exercise 3's second and third commands — the raw CEL and the match condition — and keep the messages list. The policy half still works: they read what the rules *say*, then watch two of them refuse. What they lose is meeting CEL as a language, which is the part they can read about afterwards.

Do not cut exercise 2. It is the module.
