*The problem: every practice is another thing to assemble*

A platform collects good practices: a pipeline on every push, tests and an image scan as gates, a software bill of materials, a signature that proves who built the image, a policy check before deploy, GitOps promotion, canary releases.
Each one is sound, and each one is more files, conventions and credentials a developer must get right before writing business logic.
The tenth service gets them by copying the ninth, with whatever was wrong with it.

*The golden path: the platform team's practice, as a product*

Red Hat Developer Hub, Red Hat's build of the open-source Backstage project, puts a *software template* behind a form.
This template creates two repositories: the service, and its GitOps and CI configuration.
The platform team's one-time wiring for the team, two webhooks and an Argo CD ApplicationSet, turns them into running pipelines and environments within a minute.
Keeping that template right, for every team at once, is the platform engineering team's job.

*In this demo*

. One page for the whole service: code, pipelines, deployments and what is running
. A push that becomes a tested, scanned, signed and verified deploy, with nobody writing a pipeline
. A tag that promotes the same digest to stage and then to a canary in production

== Frequently asked questions

*Where does the pipeline live, and who can change it?*::
In the service's GitOps repository, `ci/pipeline-build.yaml`, applied by Argo CD. The team can change it with a commit; the platform team improves it for new services by changing the template, and many organizations move it into a shared, versioned pipeline instead so one change reaches every service.

*What happens if a test fails or the image has a critical vulnerability?*::
The run stops at that task, nothing is committed to the GitOps repository, and the environments keep running the last image that passed.

*Why is the image not checked again at deploy time?*::
It is checked twice before Git ever names it: by Conforma in the build pipeline and again by the release pipeline before stage. A cluster admission policy can add a third check at deploy time; this workshop applies one to the trusted supply chain module's image only.

*Does the developer need to know Tekton or Argo CD?*::
No. They push code and tag releases; the template and the platform's wiring do the rest, and Developer Hub shows what happened.
